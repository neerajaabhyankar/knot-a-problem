Knot Editor

Paint, but for knots in 3D space.

Getting around

Left-drag orbits and scroll zooms. Hold ⌘ (or Ctrl) while dragging to pan — the cursor turns into a hand to tell you. F frames everything you've drawn, and clicking without dragging selects a strand.

Drawing: the live strand

One strand at a time is live — the next stroke continues it. A live strand glows like a selected one and shows a ball on each end; the brighter ball is where your next stroke will attach. Your stroke lands on the draw plane, the faint grid facing you through the orbit centre. Hold ⇧ for a straight line.

Wherever you put the pen down next, it continues from the live end — the gap between is filled in for you. You never have to aim.

Finish with Enter, Esc, a click on empty space, or by ending a stroke back near the strand's other end, which closes the loop. Finishing hands you back to Select so you can orbit and look.

Grab a handle to keep drawing — on the strand you just made or one from an hour ago, from any camera angle. What you already drew never moves. To reopen a closed loop, erase a bite out of it first.

Over and under: lift the pen

Every crossing has one strand over and one under. Two rules decide which, in this order:

  1. Lift the pen across a crossing and that strand goes under, exactly as a break means on paper. The gap that closes the loop counts too.
  2. Otherwise the strand you drew later goes over — ink drawn later sits on top of ink already on the page.

Rule 2 always applies, so nothing ever passes through itself. But it also means a shape drawn without any pen lifts is always an unknot, however tangled it looks. Breaks are what make a real knot: a trefoil is three breaks.

Linking two loops

  1. Draw a loop.
  2. Orbit about 90°, so the loop goes edge-on and reads as a line.
  3. Arm Draw and sweep a second loop around one side of that line. That's a Hopf link.

The two rules apply between different strands as well, so lifting the pen makes your stroke pass under another loop. Strands that are already clear of each other in depth are left alone.

Editing

Erase rubs away part of the strands you've selected, and only those. A bite out of a loop makes it an open arc; a rub through the middle of an arc splits it in two. [ and ] change the size.

⌘Z undoes one stroke at a time, so a bad arc can go back without taking the rest of the strand with it.
