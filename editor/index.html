Knot Editor

Paint, but for knots in 3D space.

Press D and drag to draw a strand. Lift the pen wherever it should pass under, then carry on — the gap gets filled in for you. Come back near where you started to close the loop.

Left-drag orbits, so you can see the depth you just made. Everything else is in the panel on the left.

Over and under

Lift the pen across a crossing and that strand goes under — or hold U as you draw through it, which means the same thing without breaking the ink. Unmarked, the strand you drew later goes over.

So a shape drawn without any pen lifts is never really knotted, however tangled it looks. The breaks are what tie it.

Continuing a strand

One strand at a time is live: it glows, and a ball marks the end your next stroke joins. Grab either ball to keep going — on this strand or one from an hour ago, from any angle. What you already drew never moves.

Erase a bite out of a closed loop to open it up again.

Colour and thickness

The squares beside Draw set what the next strand gets; the ones beside Select change the strands you've picked. Nine presets, or slide the hue for anything in between — every colour sits at the same saturation, so none of them will fight the rest.

Smoothing

Smooth irons the kinks out of whatever you've selected, then leaves its square open so you can slide to taste — a little cleans up a shaky line, a lot rounds a polygon off into a circle, and sliding back to 0 gives you the strand exactly as you drew it. The slider always works from that, never from the last go, so you can hunt for the right amount without it running away.

It can never pull a strand through another one, so a knot stays the knot it was — it just goes slack where it's loose and holds where it's tight.

Saving, and ready-made shapes

Save writes everything to a .knot.json file you keep. Open reads one back — or just drop the file onto the canvas. Shapes has trefoils, Hopf links and the rest, and they are ordinary saved files too.

Anything loaded arrives beside what you already have, and the view glides across to it. Nothing you drew is ever replaced — but nothing is kept between visits either, so save before you close the tab.

Analyzing, and printing a diagram

Analyze reads the scene as a knot diagram: how many crossings, which Reidemeister moves are available, the Jones polynomial, whether it differs from its mirror.

It reads it from the view you are looking at, because a crossing is a property of a projection and not of a curve — orbit and the numbers can change. Some views have no diagram at all: a ring seen exactly edge-on is a straight line. Clearest view hunts for a good one instead.

Print 2D projection saves that view as an SVG in the usual notation, with the strand that goes underneath broken at each crossing. Import reads such a picture back.

Editing

Erase only touches strands you've selected. ⌘Z undoes one stroke at a time.
